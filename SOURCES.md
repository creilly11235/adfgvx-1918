# Where else could ADFGVX messages come from

Colin Reilly asked whether any messages exist beyond the ones in Childs's log. This page records what we
found as of 11 October 2026. Short answer, Childs's Appendix B is the only large surviving set we know of.
Everything else is either a handful of worked examples or an archive lead that nobody has opened yet.

## What is held

| Source | What it holds | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Childs, *General Solution of the ADFGVX Cipher System*, Appendix B (Aegean Park Press, 2000) | 460 intercepted messages, 668 cryptograms in the logs of British and American listening posts, September to December 1918 | The main set. Every record is decoded or accounted for in this repository |
| Childs, Appendix C of the same book | The keys Childs recovered, including some that Lasry's team found as well | Used. Eight of Lasry's fourteen transposition keys match Childs's |
| Childs, *German Military Ciphers from February to November 1918* (short report with Friedman's 1934 foreword, printed 1935, declassified by NSA) | Two worked examples, one of them under a complete 18 column key that appears nowhere else | Used as a key source, no new operational messages |
| Childs's 1919 history (a 218 page scan from the National Cryptologic Museum) and the museum's DKP005 016 bundle of RICHI key sheets and code examples | Narrative, key tables and examples | Read for keys. We found no second copy of any message in them |
| Moorman, *Final Report of the Radio Intelligence Section* (1935) | How intercepts were forwarded in October 1918, including ADFGVX copies sent to General Headquarters | Read. It shows that copies were made, not where they ended up |

## Leads that have not been opened

Lasry's thesis says that most of the intercepted material was not preserved, and that British Army and German
records for 1918 were largely destroyed. These are the places a surviving copy could still sit. A catalogue that
returns nothing is a limit of the catalogue, not proof that nothing is there.

| Place | Reference | What we know |
|---|---|---|
| US National Archives, College Park | Record Group 120 Entries 104, 105 and 106 | AEF radio intelligence records, not yet available online. Box level contents are unknown |
| University of Virginia, Childs papers | Accession 9256 b, Box 14 | Two items on German ciphers dated November 1918, plus whatever else the box holds. Scans were requested, not received |
| National Cryptologic Museum library | DK 055 069, notes on the RICHI ADFGVX cipher dated 20 November 1918 | A catalogue record without an attachment |
| UK National Archives | HW 7/35 and HW 3/176 to 182 | HW 7/35 is an anonymous history of MI 1B written between 1919 and 1923. It is not digitised and it is not an intercept log. The catalogue has no ADFGVX keyword hit |
| French Service historique de la Défense, Vincennes | GR 19 N 1416 to 1418, Section du chiffre | New lead. The catalogue says it holds French and German cipher grids and deciphering reports. Not opened yet because the site was down for maintenance |
| French Service historique de la Défense, Vincennes | GR 7 N 1267 and GR 6 N 21, plus the radio direction finding diary 26 N 28/9 | Inconclusive. GR 7 N 1267 has wireless correspondence between the Allies, August 1914 to April 1919, and the descriptions do not mention ciphers |
| Gallica | Searches for ADFGVX, ADFGX and Painvin with cryptography | The narrow searches were read in full and returned only secondary works such as de Lastours, no intercept logs. Three broad searches were read to their first pages only and are still to be finished |
| Authors of the 2017 paper | Their own transcription and decryption archive | We asked once and have not had a reply. Lasry's per message list of what was read has never been published |

## Messages from outside the log

Georges Painvin broke the first ADFGVX traffic in June 1918, but those intercepts were French and are not in
Childs's log. Their cryptograms are cited in histories such as Kahn's *The Codebreakers* only in small numbers.
We have not found a transcribed set of them.

## Reports we did not use

Several 2026 news and blog items claim an AI system read a message from 24 November 1918. They trace back to a
single post by a writer using the name Prinz, who used a key from Childs's 1919 typescript. That is the message
on page 217 in our tables, and we count it as read by others. We did not use the news items as sources.

## Search for a list of what was read in 2017

On 11 October 2026 we looked for any per message list of which records the 2017 team read. We found none.
Checked, with the outcome for each.

| Place | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Schmeh's February 2017 post and all its comments | Gives the team's key table with a total of 618 cryptograms recovered, plus Schmeh's list of 22 unsolved entries. No list of the records that were read |
| Schmeh's separate page of unsolved ADFGVX messages | Already used. It names unsolved entries only, so every other record is unknown, not known read |
| Lasry's thesis chapter on ADFGVX | Gives the counts and the fourteen keys. No per message list, and 50 unread of 668 is the only total |
| The Cryptologia paper of 2017 | Not available to us in full text, so its supplements were not checked. This is an access gap |
| Lasry's 2015 Kassel talk slides | The file text could not be read, so it is unchecked |
| General web search for a Lasry repository, data set or CrypTool material holding the plaintexts | No hit. A search engine miss is not proof that none exists |

This search is thin, not complete. Until a per message list turns up, no record of ours outside the 22 entries can be called new,
and the fill leads stay leads.
